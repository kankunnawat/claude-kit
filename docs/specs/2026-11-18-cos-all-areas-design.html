1 · Today

The chief of staff only works in Hausback

The skill calls one session per project the chief of staff, but that session runs lanes, so it is really a lead. Hausback's daily-branch and tracker steps are written into the generic text, so no other project can use it.

You also get one "Needs you" list per project, not one queue.

2 · The change

One chief of staff, a lead per area, one queue

The chief of staff does no area work. It keeps the roster, builds the queue, relays your answers and reports twice a day. A lead turns work into open pull requests with a fresh review and evidence. Nobody new can merge.

3 · How it runs

From your word to a merged change

  1. 1You give work to the chief of staff or straight to an area's lead.
  2. 2The lead starts a worker in its own worktree, then a fresh reviewer.
  3. 3The lead opens the pull request the way that repo's own rules say.
  4. 4The unblock pass puts it in your one queue, with screenshots for anything you can see.
  5. 5You clear the queue in a batch; the chief of staff relays each answer.
  6. 6The merge agent lands it where your guardrails allow, as today.

Risk → answer

Moving Hausback's text breaks its loop when you resume it.
Its rules move word for word into its own manual. The proof walks /lead in hausback-core and checks it finds the manual and stops on the hold.
One queue mixes projects and files an item on the wrong list.
The queue is a view rebuilt at each pass. Each item stays in its own thread, pull request or that area's own to-do list.
A lead starts doing things in AlphaOS or Agentic Discovery that nobody asked for.
A watch lead only takes work you give it, follows that repo's own gate, and never merges. Starting a lead needs your yes per area.
The scheduled reports fire with the old prompts during the change.
The prompts are rewritten last and read back. Until then they still check the Hausback hold and stop.
