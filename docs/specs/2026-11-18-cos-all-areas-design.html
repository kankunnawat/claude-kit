Approve: one chief of staff over every area, with a lead in each

The chief of staff keeps a roster and one queue of what needs you, synced through your two brain repos so it works from any machine; each area's lead runs the work there, Bitazza included.

1 · Today

The chief of staff only works in Hausback

The skill calls one session per project the chief of staff, but that session runs lanes, so it is really a lead. Hausback's daily-branch and tracker steps are written into the generic text, so no other project can use it.

You also get one "Needs you" list per project, and its rules sit in a folder on one Mac with no git and no sync.

2 · The change

One chief of staff, a lead per area, one queue

The chief of staff does no area work. It keeps the roster, builds the queue, relays your answers and reports twice a day. A lead turns work into open pull requests with a fresh review and evidence. Nobody new can merge.

Bitazza areas join through a second roster in the work brain. Their leads run on the work provider.

Names: /cos /lead kan-brain/cos/roster.md btz-brain/cos/roster.md

3 · How it runs

From your word to a merged change

  1. 1You give work to the chief of staff or to an area's lead, or name open work for the lead to adopt.
  2. 2The lead asks the machine how many sessions it has room for, starts that many workers in their own worktrees, then a fresh reviewer for each.
  3. 3The lead opens the pull request the way that repo's own rules say.
  4. 4The unblock pass puts it in your one queue, with screenshots for anything you can see.
  5. 5You clear the queue in a batch; the chief of staff relays each answer.
  6. 6The merge agent lands it where your guardrails allow, as today.

Risk → answer

Moving Hausback's text breaks its loop when you resume it.
The first commit is a pure move, checked word for word. On "resume hausback" its lead starts inside the Hausback project with its own timed reports and the inbox pointed at it; the spec lists the six steps.
Bitazza content leaks into the personal brain, or personal notes reach a work session.
Each area's rows, manual and record stay in its own brain. A Bitazza lead reads only the work brain and runs on the work provider. The queue shows a Bitazza item as one line with a link: no code, customer data or secrets.
New sessions pile onto a Mac that is already full, or work sits idle while the Mac has room.
Every dispatch first asks one script how many sessions may start now. It reads load, swap, memory, disk and the busy heavy-check slots for the whole Mac, so all areas share one budget. Today it would answer 0.
Two machines each run a chief of staff.
The synced roster names one holder and its machine. A second machine says who holds the role and stops, until you say "take over".
One queue mixes projects and files an item on the wrong list.
The queue is a view rebuilt at each pass. Each item stays in its own thread, pull request or that area's own to-do list.
A lead starts doing things in AlphaOS or Agentic Discovery that nobody asked for.
A watch lead lists the area's open pull requests and threads and asks which to adopt. It adopts none unasked, follows that repo's own gate, and never merges.
The scheduled reports fire with the old prompts during the change.
The prompts are rewritten last and read back. Until then they still check the Hausback hold and stop.

What changed while building

A watch lead keeps an area file.
/lead creates the area's file with its current state and log when the area has none. A watch area still needs no manual, but its lead's state must sync like every other.
In watch mode the worker opens the PR.
The lead then records the reviewer's verdict on it in the repo's own format. The design said the lead opens it in one place and the worker in another.
The roster gained a quiet-hours line and a Machines table.
Quiet hours use Hausback's clock (00:00 to 08:00 Bangkok) for every area until you say otherwise. The table holds each machine's budget numbers and local folder. The work roster carries a copy, because a work-side lead never opens the main roster.
The chief of staff has its own hold file.
It sits in a local folder on this Mac. It pauses the chief of staff's reports and puts no area on hold.
Scheduled task ids are stored nowhere.
Each report run reads the holder line and stops when it names another thread, so a pair left behind on another machine goes quiet by itself.
cos-launch.sh --dry-run exits 0 on a full machine.
It prints the budget and "a real start would be refused now". A real start is still refused.
cos-log.py stamps the date as well as the time.
An area's log spans days.
The retro has no reading taken 10 minutes after a launch.
It compares the load each launch logged with the budget lines the lead logs at later wakes.
One line of the Hausback manual was reworded.
A work repo's name became "work repos" in the board rule, so the separation check prints nothing.
The queue page does not exist yet.
The first report creates it and writes its link into the roster.

Already decided

  • One queue for you, across every area.
  • Picking up ticket batches is optional and only Hausback uses it.
  • The status page is the queue's first home.
  • No lead starts when this lands. The Mac is already full: load 20.3 on 10 cores when measured.

  • Synced state lives in kan-brain, and Bitazza areas in btz-brain.
