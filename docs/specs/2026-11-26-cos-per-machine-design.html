1 · Today

2 · The change

Each Mac gets its own chief of staff and its own list

Each one lists only what you can clear from that Mac, and ends with one line for the other: "on the other Mac: n waiting, oldest so long". It reads that line from the synced record and never acts on it.

The work Mac stays the home: it keeps the roster, the retro, work areas and areas with no lead.

Risk → answer

You get two reports and two lists instead of one.
This is the cost of the shape. Each list ends with a count line for the other Mac, so nothing there is out of sight.
An answer crosses Macs as text in a synced file, and a lead acts on it.
Not allowed. A chief of staff that gets an answer for the other Mac says where it belongs and records nothing. Your typed answer on the Mac that owns the item is the only consent.
Two sessions edit one roster file.
Each pulls before an edit, pushes at once, and writes only its own cells and its own areas' rows. A refused push is pulled and tried again, never forced.
The second chief of staff never starts, or its Mac sleeps.
Then nothing gets worse than today: the home one keeps listing those items and you keep pasting. A stale count line shows its time.
Work-side names reach the personal Mac.
The home Mac's banned list is not copied. The new one drafts its own list from its own areas and shows it to you before its first page.

For you to decide

  1. One queue page per Mac, or one merged page?I recommend one per Mac: each page has a single writer and shows what you can clear from that Mac. A merged page needs both to write the same page, and a work-side line could not be built on the personal Mac.
  2. Does the evening retro run on the work Mac only, or on each Mac?I recommend the work Mac only: it reads both Macs' records from the brain, so each proposal reaches you once.
